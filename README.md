Problem Statement
The East African Community (EAC) consists of eight Partner States. Among them, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and South Sudan are particularly affected by ongoing conflict, instability, and post-conflict recovery, leading to large-scale displacement of populations across borders.
Uganda is the largest refugee-hosting country in the region, accommodating hundreds of thousands of displaced individuals, including school-aged children and youth. A significant challenge these refugee-displaced students face is the loss and inaccessibility of their academic documents, which severely limits their ability to continue their education or pursue employment opportunities.
In both their countries of origin and Uganda as a host country, many schools that refugee-displaced students attend continue to use paper-based systems for storing academic records, such as transcripts and certificates. These documents are physically stored in administrators' offices and are often not digitized or accessible remotely. Over 80% of schools in refugee-displaced school environments (whether in home conflict zones or refugee camps) rely on paper-based record-keeping, with 90% of schools in Uganda’s refugee settlements following this method. Once students leave these environments, accessing these documents becomes nearly impossible.
This issue is particularly acute in refugee camps such as Nakivale and Kyangwali, where students often complete their education without receiving original academic documents. If they are relocated or resettled, they lose all access to these records. Due to the absence of digitized systems or centralized retrieval portals, retrieving these documents is extremely difficult.
From 2022 to 2023, I conducted an ethnographic study with over 3,000 refugee-displaced students across various environments in Uganda, including Nakivale, Kyangwali, and Kampala. The findings were alarming:
In Nakivale, 900 out of 1,000 students had lost access to their academic documents.


In Kyangwali, 600 out of 800 students faced the same issue.


In Kampala, 300 urban refugee youth, especially those who studied in refugee camps or their home countries, reported lacking evidence of their education.


Across all these environments, the document retrieval rate was found to be less than 5%. This low retrieval rate is due to students' geographic displacement and school administrators' lack of digital infrastructure to manage records. The current manual retrieval process is inefficient, insecure, expensive, and unreliable, involving informal networks, middlemen, phone calls, and paper archives.
The impact is severe. Without access to their academic records, refugee-displaced youth are excluded from scholarship opportunities, denied reentry to school, and blocked from formal employment. Many are willing to pay for a secure, affordable system to retrieve their lost documents, but no such solution currently exists.
In conclusion, the overreliance on paper-based academic record systems—both in students' countries of origin and in Ugandan refugee settlements—combined with the unpredictable nature of displacement, has created a systemic educational barrier for thousands of refugee-displaced students. Immediate action is needed to digitize academic records and create a centralized, secure platform for document retrieval, or an entire generation of displaced learners risks being left behind.
Solution
The Tshijuka RDP platform provides a technology-driven solution to help refugee-displaced students securely and efficiently retrieve their lost academic documents, regardless of their location. Using AI-powered chatbots, cloud computing for data storage, and robust cybersecurity for data protection, our platform connects schools' paper-based record-keeping systems to a digital ecosystem.
The solution includes three user-friendly, role-based interfaces:
Refugee Interface:


Submit document retrieval requests


Upload ID or use AI facial recognition for identity verification


Track requests in real time (pending, in progress, or completed)


Download verified documents


Schools Interface:


Receive and review document requests


Upload verified academic documents


Admin Panel:


Automatically monitor all requests and user activity on the platform


By integrating existing school management systems with our platform, we offer a seamless solution that addresses the challenges faced by refugee-displaced students and school administrators, ensuring greater access to academic records and educational opportunities for refugee youth.
